Understanding MACRS Convention Standards

Conventions determine when during the tax year an asset is treated as being placed in service or disposed of, establishing the deduction allowed for the first and final tax years:

  • Half-Year Convention: Assumes the vehicle was placed in service (or disposed of) exactly in the middle of the tax year. This allows a half-year of depreciation in Year 1 and spreads a 5-year asset recovery over 6 calendar tax years.
  • Mid-Quarter Convention: Required by the IRS if more than 40% of the total cost basis of all depreciable personal property acquired during the year was placed in service during the 4th quarter (Q4).
Understanding MACRS Depreciation Methods

Depreciation Methods define the mathematical rate at which the vehicle's tax basis is written off over its 5-year class life:

  • 200% Declining Balance (200DB): Front-loads write-offs by doubling the straight-line rate, switching automatically to straight-line when that method yields an equal or greater deduction. Standard for passenger autos and trucks.
  • 150% Declining Balance (150DB): Offers accelerated write-offs at 1.5 times straight-line rates. Often used under Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) rules or for alternative business asset elections.
  • Straight Line Method (SL): Spreads deductions evenly across the full recovery period, minimizing initial write-offs to preserve deductions for future higher-income tax years.
